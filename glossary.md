# Glossary

The vocabulary used across this repository, defined plainly.

## Generative Engine Optimization

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of structuring content so an AI answer engine — a chat assistant, an AI overview panel, a voice assistant — can read it, understand it, and cite it in a generated answer. It sits alongside traditional search engine optimization but targets a different reader: a model that extracts and paraphrases a passage, not a person scanning a ranked list of links.

## AI visibility

AI visibility is the likelihood that a page is cited, quoted, or otherwise surfaced by an AI answer engine when it composes a response — the unit behind the percentage gains reported throughout the criteria table (for example, on G1, G7, G9, and G11). It is a measure of appearing inside a generated answer, not of ranking position or click-through traffic, which is what traditional search visibility measures.

## Citable capsule

A citable capsule is a short, self-contained block of text — a paragraph, a list, a definition — that answers one specific question completely on its own. An answer engine can lift a citable capsule out of a page and quote it without needing the surrounding context. Content built from clear, separate capsules is easier to cite than one long, meandering paragraph that never fully resolves any single question.

## Modular structure (Ranch-Style)

Modular structure, also called Ranch-Style in this repository, is a page organized so each H2 section stands entirely on its own — zero cross-references, no pointing back ("as mentioned above") or forward ("see below") to another section. The name borrows from ranch-style architecture: a single-story house where every room opens onto a shared hallway, rather than a multi-story one where reaching a room means passing through another first. An AI answer engine that lifts one section out of a page can use it immediately, with no other section required for context.

## Query fan-out

When an AI answer engine turns one user question into several related searches before composing its reply, that expansion is called query fan-out. A single question about a product might fan out into separate searches for price, reviews, and shipping. A page that answers the fan-out questions, not just the original one, is more likely to be pulled into the final answer.

## llms.txt

llms.txt is a plain-text file placed at the root of a website to give AI crawlers a direct, structured summary of the site's content — similar in spirit to robots.txt or sitemap.xml, but written for language models rather than search-engine crawlers. Its presence signals that a site was built with answer engines in mind.
